## Problem

Individuals who hold Bitcoin to achieve long-term capital gains will likely experience life-altering events – even more so in today’s unpredictable world with major challenges like wars, global warming, and the AI revolution.
Situations followed by economic hardship force holders to sell their Bitcoin to stay financially afloat when necessary, losing the chance to realize Bitcoin’s potential over the long run.

## Solution

Let’s look at the [Boro.today](https://boro.today) service.
Boro provides the infrastructure to borrow cash (take out a loan) against your own Bitcoin.

**How does it work?** <br/>
Users hold Bitcoin on a platform that uses Boro's infrastructure. Instead of selling their Bitcoin, they borrow USDC (which they can use however they wish) by locking their Bitcoin as collateral.

The idea for the service belongs to Bitwala’s CEO, Dennis Daiber. It was conceived as a standalone B2B tool that—with a few well-written APIs—could be integrated into any crypto platform or Bitcoin wallet.
As Boro's first client, Bitwala integrated the service into their app, wrapping it in a custom front end with a Bitwala-branded UI and an enhanced UX.

## Phase 1

With the opt-in flow already in staging, I began by testing it myself and conducting user research.
Observing and listening to users gave me a clearer picture of how the flow should look and what changes were needed in the existing design.

Because the service was accessible only to registered users holding actual Bitcoin, my research also encompassed the steps preceding the loan flow:
**Sign Up & Login**, **Dashboard Hierarchy & Intuitiveness**, and **MPC Wallet Creation**.

## Phase 2

The first round of user testing demonstrated a completion rate of only **~12%** for the "Loan Taking" flow.
This was due to micro-friction points during account setup that, at scale, completely blocked users from taking out a loan.
To increase transaction success, we needed to make the experience more intuitive and the interface easier to navigate—especially since modern product flows must be fast and seamless.

Alongside the development of the **Loan Management** and **Repayment** APIs, I initiated a round of user research.
The insights pointed toward focusing on the following areas:

- Home (Dashboard/Overview) UX Architecture
- Crashes & Error Handling
- Handling Unexpected Steps in the Loan Journey
- Wallet-to-Wallet Transfer Messaging (from Custodial to MPC)
- Fee Transparency
- Login & Onboarding UX

How the Borrow flow looked after the redesign:

## Outcomes

The second round of user testing showed a dramatic increase in the "Loan Taking" success rate, with **~75%** of transactions successfully completed following the redesign.

While the "Repayment" flow achieved a **~20%** success rate—still a notable improvement over the initial benchmark—we identified key areas for improvement that will be crucial in the next development phases.
